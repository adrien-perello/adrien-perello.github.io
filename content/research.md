## The problem

Sustainability decisions about emerging chemicals and materials are often needed before the underlying evidence is complete. Process descriptions are fragmented. Quantities are estimated from different sources and models. Assumptions change as a technology develops. A plausible final inventory table can therefore conceal incompatible evidence, unresolved alternatives or results that became stale after revision.

My research asks how these partial decisions can be converted into prospective life-cycle models early enough to guide design, while remaining open to review and correction.

## Reviewable LCA reconstruction

The scientific spine is **reviewable LCA reconstruction under incomplete and changing evidence**. Reconstruction covers the work between source material and a computable foreground inventory: defining process structure, interpreting observations, deriving quantities, selecting background links, separating alternatives and preserving the dependencies that determine what must be reconsidered when something changes.

The objective is not automation for its own sake. It is earlier and more defensible decision support, with consequential assumptions and limitations visible to human reviewers.

## Benchmark and evaluation programme

The broader programme is concerned with how AI-supported reconstruction should be tested, compared and challenged. This includes:

1. operational definitions of reconstruction tasks and failure classes;
2. shared evidence packets and independently adjudicated cases;
3. evaluation of evidence fidelity, physical and methodological coherence, and revision integrity;
4. human-review outcomes such as appropriate reliance, workload and abstention; and
5. decision-support performance under fixed evidence, time and expert-attention budgets.

The benchmark must remain architecture-neutral. A simpler table-centred workflow should be able to outperform a richer representation if it produces equally dependable decisions with less burden.

## A candidate representational hypothesis

The **Candidate Inventories Model (CIM)** is one proposed representation for reconstruction state. It aims to keep evidence, interpretations, quantities, background links, alternatives, checks and revisions connected while an inventory is assembled and revised.

CIM is not the research programme, the benchmark or a validated product. It is a falsifiable candidate that should be simplified, replaced or rejected if another representation supports review and revision more effectively.

## Application domain

The main application is trustworthy AI-enabled Safe-and-Sustainable-by-Design and prospective-LCA decision support for chemical and material innovation. Current work takes place in the EU-funded INSIGHT project. Case studies provide demanding tests of incomplete evidence, functional equivalence and changing assumptions. Detailed benchmark material and comparative findings will be published only when the relevant evidence and disclosure gates have been passed.

## Open questions for collaboration

- Which reconstruction failures are consequential for a stated LCA use, and how can they be adjudicated reproducibly?
- How should a system distinguish deterministic recalculation from changes that require renewed expert judgment?
- When does additional provenance improve review, and when does it create avoidable burden?
- How should human review and appropriate reliance be evaluated alongside model accuracy?
- Can architecture-neutral challenges make prospective-LCA systems more comparable and externally contestable?

Relevant collaboration centres on bounded research objects: benchmark cases, evaluation protocols, alternative representations, adjudication methods and demanding SSbD decision problems.
